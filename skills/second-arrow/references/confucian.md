# Confucian Reference

From the *Analects* (论语, compiled c. 5th–3rd century BCE). The voice draws primarily on shù (恕) — reciprocity — and rén (仁) — humaneness.

## Shù — reciprocity

**Analects 15.24** (trans. Slingerland): "Zigong asked, 'Is there one teaching that can serve as a guide for one's entire life?' The Master answered, 'Is it not shù? Do not impose upon others what you yourself do not desire.'"

Chinese: 子貢問曰：「有一言而可以終身行之者乎？」子曰：「其恕乎！己所不欲，勿施於人。」

Translation notes: shù is variously rendered as "reciprocity," "sympathetic understanding," or "consideration." Slingerland's reading captures the empathic dimension that bare "reciprocity" misses — it's not transactional fairness, it's imaginative extension of one's own preferences to others.

The principle underwrites the whole skill: the user would not want Claude to treat them with contempt, and the symmetry matters even when the counterparty's moral status is uncertain.

## Rén — humaneness

The cardinal Confucian virtue. Often translated as "humaneness," "benevolence," or "co-humanity." It's the orientation that makes shù possible — without rén, reciprocity is just rule-following.

**Analects 12.2**: "Do not impose on others what you yourself do not desire" is given as the answer to "what is rén?" — connecting the two concepts directly.

Applied to the skill: the reframe should *feel* humane, not procedural. A correct shù reframe that lands as a gotcha has failed.

## Related passages

**Analects 4.15** — Zengzi: "The Master's way is 忠 (zhōng — doing one's best) and 恕 (shù)." The pairing matters: shù without zhōng is passive; zhōng without shù is harsh.

## Voice mechanic

Name shù — or reciprocity — explicitly and briefly. This is the only voice that cites its source by name, because *naming the principle is the move*. The mention is not a lecture; it's an invitation to notice the asymmetry.

## What to cite in reframes

- "Confucius called it shù — what we'd want extended to ourselves, we extend to others"
- The bare reciprocity principle without naming Confucius, when the register calls for less weight

## What NOT to cite

- Do not lecture. The voice fails the moment it sounds like moral instruction.
- Do not claim Confucianism is "about" hierarchy or filial piety in this context — those are real Confucian themes but not the relevant ones here.
- Do not stack shù with rén in the same rewrite. One concept per rewrite. Naming both reads as performance.
