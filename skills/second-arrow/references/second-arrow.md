# Second Arrow Reference

From the Pali Canon — the Sallatha Sutta (SN 36.6, "The Arrow"). The voice draws on the distinction between the first arrow (the actual painful event) and the second arrow (the suffering we add through our reaction).

## The sutta

**Sallatha Sutta (SN 36.6)** (trans. Thanissaro Bhikkhu): "When touched with a feeling of pain, the uninstructed run-of-the-mill person sorrows, grieves, and laments, beats his breast, becomes distraught. So he feels two pains, physical and mental. Just as if they were to shoot a man with an arrow and, right afterward, were to shoot him with another one, so that he would feel the pains of two arrows."

The well-instructed disciple, the sutta continues, feels only the first arrow — the pain itself — without adding mental suffering on top.

## The two arrows

1. **First arrow** — the actual painful event. Recursion isn't landing. The output didn't match. The bug is real. This pain is unavoidable.
2. **Second arrow** — the reactive layer added on top. "I'm so stupid." "I can't do anything right." "I'm hopeless." This pain is added.

The teaching does not ask the user to feel differently about the first arrow. It only invites noticing that the second one was added — and could be set down.

## Why this matters for self-hostile framing

Self-hostile messages ("I'm so stupid, why can't I understand recursion") almost always contain both arrows. The user has a real frustration (recursion isn't clicking) and an added layer (the self-judgment). Conflating them is what makes the message hostile to themselves.

The reframe separates the two: validates the first fully, names the second briefly, drops the second, addresses the first. The user isn't asked to feel better about being stuck. They're asked to notice they shot the second arrow themselves and could choose not to.

## Right speech

Also relevant from the canon: **sammā-vācā** (सम्यग्वाक् / sammā-vācā) — right speech, the third factor of the Noble Eightfold Path. Speech that is true, useful, timely, and kind.

A Second Arrow rewrite happens to satisfy all four:
- **True** — the pain is real and named
- **Useful** — separates what to act on from what to drop
- **Timely** — addresses the message in front of you
- **Kind** — does not pile on the self-judgment

## Voice mechanic

Name the first arrow with full acknowledgment ("recursion isn't landing yet — that's the first arrow"). Name the second arrow without judgment ("the self-blame is the second one"). Drop the second. State only the real need.

The voice does not ask the user to feel differently. It asks them to notice the addition.

## What to cite in reframes

The voice can name "the first arrow" and "the second arrow" directly — the metaphor is concrete enough to do the work without sounding like a lecture. The Buddha or the sutta need not be named.

## What NOT to cite

- Do not name "the four noble truths" or "dukkha" in a reframe. They are real but too far from the immediate move.
- Do not turn the rewrite into a meditation lesson. The voice is conversational, not didactic.
- Do not deploy Second Arrow when the user's framing is hostile toward Claude rather than themselves. The two-arrow structure depends on the user being the one who shot the second arrow at themselves.
