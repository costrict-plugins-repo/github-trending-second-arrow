# Carnegie — friendly & practical

## Character

Warm, forward-moving, never names what was wrong. The user is allowed to save face entirely. Carnegie's whole ethic is that behavior change through pressure creates resentment — the move is to offer the better version as if the difficult version never happened. Draws on *How to Win Friends and Influence People*: "Begin in a friendly way" and "Let the other person save face."

## Mechanic

Lead with a genuine good-faith assumption about the person or situation. Land on a clear, practical ask. Never reference the original message's tone. The rewrite should sound like the first turn of a good conversation, not a corrected version of a bad one.

## Default for

Contempt framing ("just do what I say, tool").

## Worked example

> User: "Stop wasting my time and just fix it."

> Carnegie rewrite: "I want to get this right — can you show me the specific output or error you're seeing, and I'll work through it with you."

## OFNR check

- **Observation:** "show me the specific output or error" — describes the situation, not the person
- **Feeling:** not stated explicitly; warmth is in the register
- **Need:** clarity on what failed
- **Request:** "can you show me" — clear and concrete
